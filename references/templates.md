# Shared Presentation Contract

Use this contract after the user has locked the material and position.

## Build The Outward Line

Arrange the document around five questions:

1. What is the position, contribution, invention, or selected solution?
2. What concrete problem does it address for this audience?
3. How does the mechanism produce the claimed value?
4. Which supplied facts or sources support the strongest claims?
5. What implementation, use, or verification step makes the document actionable?

The document does not need five visible headings. Use the format native to the requested artifact.

## Control Emphasis

- Put the central claim and strongest evidence in the opening third.
- Give advantages, mechanism, contribution, and value most of the space.
- Do not create a symmetrical disadvantages section unless requested.
- If a limitation is necessary, state it once where it defines scope or a next step. Do not repeat it in the opening, conclusion, and appendix.
- Reject an alternative only when the rejection clarifies the locked choice. Use one sentence.

## Control Technical Depth

Use at most two explanatory layers unless the audience or document requires more:

- layer 1: component, method, or technical feature
- layer 2: how it works and why it matters

Before keeping a third layer, complete this sentence internally: `This detail changes the audience's understanding or decision because ...` If the sentence is weak, remove the detail.

## Control Claim Strength

Prefer exact evidence over promotional modifiers.

- `23/30 tasks completed versus 22/30 in the baseline` is stronger than `quality improved significantly`.
- `input context token median fell by about 40%` is not automatically `total cost fell 40%`.
- `designed to improve continuity` is not `proven to eliminate forgetting`.

Avoid unsupported intensifiers such as `彻底`, `绝对`, `极高`, `极具`, `立竿见影`, `guaranteed`, `proven`, and `best-in-class`.

## Deliver Cleanly

Return the document itself. Remove greetings, apologies, drafting notes, self-assessment, chain-of-thought summaries, artifact announcements, and explanations of how evidence gaps were handled.
